SFT validation loss at steps 50/100/150/200/244: 0.424, 0.329, 0.295, 0.276, 0.273 (RFT r1 at 50/100/150/200/258:
0.410, 0.326, 0.287, 0.275, 0.271). 86\% of each training row is the fixed system prompt (about 1,280 of 1,490 tokens),
so rows have nearly equal length; batch size 2 runs out of memory on a T4, mostly due to the 262k-token vocabulary.

\paragraph{GRPO probe (no-go).} With the SFT adapter, 8 first turns were sampled at $T{=}1$ for 377 training prompts in
the SFT error areas (230 ask-first, 147 block-card). 95.7\% of samples succeeded and only 12.7\% of prompts showed any
reward variance, with 1 prompt failing on all 8, so GRPO was not run on single-turn data.

\paragraph{Stage 2 sampling.} From the round-1 RFT model, 8 conversations per prompt at $T{=}1$ on 482 new training
language-switch prompts (2.3 minutes on one L40S with vLLM): 60.6\% of prompts had reward variance and 63.9\% had at
least one fully correct sample. Switching into English stayed the hardest case (30 of 122 prompts with a correct
sample, against 61 of 74 into Bengali). After the length filter, 289 samples were kept, giving 3,410 training rows with
1,067 grounding examples. RFT r2's validation loss is higher than r1's (0.292 vs.\ 0.271) because the validation
set is the round-1 distribution and a third of the new rows are grounding examples; every scored test improved
(Section~\ref{sec:rl}), so we do not read validation loss across different training mixes.

\paragraph{Multi-turn GRPO.} From RFT r2, online GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} on the same training
language-switch prompts. Each step samples 8 full turn-2 conversations for each of 16 prompts at $T{=}1$ through the
evaluation's agent loop and mock bank, scores them with \tool{reward\_lang}, normalises rewards within each group of 8
(groups with no variance are skipped) and takes one AdamW step (lr $10^{-5}$, LoRA weights only) on
$-A\cdot$ mean log-probability of the model's own turn-2 tokens; the prefix conversation and tool results are masked.
Updates are fully on-policy, so the PPO ratio is 1, and there is no KL term. 25 steps cover 400 of the 482 prompts
once: 63 minutes on one A100 (bf16, about \$2.60). About half of the groups had reward variance at every step. Per-step
training reward was flat within noise (1.315 for steps 0--7, 1.293 for 17--24), because every step sees new prompts;
the fixed evaluation sets measure the change (Section~\ref{sec:rl}). Every sampled conversation is published with
its reward parts for auditing: no echoing, reply length unchanged (129 to 131 characters), and three single-step
spikes of wrong actions (12--15\%) that did not persist and do not appear in evaluation.
